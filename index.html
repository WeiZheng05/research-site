Research profile

My research develops human-centered, domain-grounded AI through interdisciplinary and cross-institutional collaboration. Rather than treating AI as a black-box product, we build modular, reusable pipelines that combine data, disciplinary knowledge, uncertainty quantification, and human expertise—while keeping people meaningfully in control of judgment and decisions.

Three connected research pillars organize the work:

  1. AI-powered teaching and learning support — an integrated NSF program (Targeted Infusion + Excellence in Research) that elicits authentic student thinking and grounds AI assessment and adaptive feedback in disciplinary rubrics, with instructor oversight.
  2. Human-centered AI for disciplinary and ethical decisions — an emerging direction using dual knowledge graphs, RAG, and LLMs to help professionals and students evaluate AI advice and deliberate under uncertainty across cultural and institutional contexts.
  3. Civil infrastructure prediction and decisions under uncertainty — MarTREC-supported transformer forecasting of bridge deterioration, building on earlier Bayesian inference, scour, and reliability research for risk-informed inspection and maintenance.

The unifying question: how can AI combine data, disciplinary knowledge, uncertainty, and human expertise so that learning, professional judgment, and engineering decisions remain rigorous—and humans stay in control?

Research pillar · Civil infrastructure under uncertainty

From sequence data
to forecasts and uncertainty.

In our recent journal paper, Yuzhong Huang and I formulate corrosion-initiation probability prediction as a multi-horizon sequence-learning problem: use a history of deterioration-related information to estimate how risk evolves over future time steps. This work evidences transformer-based predictive modeling, probabilistic/Bayesian ML traditions, uncertainty quantification, and risk-informed inspection and maintenance thinking.

1. Organize sequence data

A diffusion-based stochastic reliability model generates controlled corrosion-initiation probability trajectories. Historical sequences, scenario variables, and future or scenario-defined inputs form the forecasting task. This simulation testbed prepares a structure for future inspection, sensor, laboratory, and exposure histories.

2. Predict future trajectories

A Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) learns the relationship between sequence history, covariates, and future probability trajectories. Its attention and variable-selection structure supports interpretable multi-horizon forecasting. The study compares TFT with recurrent and other predictive baselines.

3. Quantify uncertainty

Stochastic reliability modeling represents uncertainty in the simulated deterioration process. Repeated predictions with MC Dropout estimate approximate model-level epistemic uncertainty, reporting predictive means, dispersion, and approximate 95% predictive intervals.

Connecting prediction to decisions

The aim is to help engineers interpret both forecast risk and confidence in the prediction when comparing inspection needs, maintenance timing, and infrastructure management options. This continues my research on Bayesian inference, reliability, and decision-making under uncertainty.

The published study is a simulation-based proof of concept. Its MC Dropout intervals describe approximate model uncertainty within that setting, rather than a complete or field-calibrated account of all deterioration uncertainty. Field data validation remains necessary before operational use.

Research in Progress

Active studies and emerging directions, ordered to surface ethics, human-centered AI for learning emotions and emotional intelligence, future-workforce skills in the AI era, and transformer-based civil-infrastructure prediction—before broader AI assessment validation, evidence synthesis, and knowledge-grounded decision support.

1. Human Evaluation of AI-Generated Ethical Reasoning in Engineering Decision Scenarios

This study investigates the quality, completeness, reliability, and limitations of AI-generated ethical reasoning in engineering and professional decision scenarios. The current evaluation includes 12 scenarios with three independently generated AI responses per scenario, producing 36 formal reasoning samples for human review. Expert reviewers evaluate ethical problem framing, stakeholder and public-welfare considerations, professional responsibility and accountability, fairness and cultural relevance, alternative courses of action, justification, and AI-specific risks. The study is designed to identify where AI can usefully augment ethical deliberation and where human judgment remains essential.

2. AI-Generated Adaptive Feedback for Learning Emotions, Emotional Awareness, and Emotion Regulation

This collaborative research examines how AI-generated adaptive feedback may support students’ learning emotions, emotional awareness, emotion regulation, and related emotional-intelligence capabilities during learning.

The current dataset includes students’ responses from learning planning, homework, quizzes, and post-learning reflection. It combines structured ratings with open-ended explanations of students’ emotions, learning experiences, and strategies for regulating emotion.

Current analysis examines patterns, strengths, and difficulties in students’ emotional awareness and regulation. A related AI component generates individualized feedback and support from students’ open-ended responses and investigates whether such feedback is appropriate, useful, and aligned with human educational judgment.

Connection: learning emotions → emotional awareness → emotion regulation → AI-generated adaptive feedback → human evaluation → support for learning and emotional-intelligence-related development.

3. Human-Aligned AI-Powered Adaptive Learning Support for AI-Era Soft Skills in Engineering Education: Emotional Intelligence, Communication, Creativity, and Ethical Reasoning

Abstract accepted for ASEE CIEC 2027; full paper in preparation.

This research examines how human-aligned AI can support the professional and higher-order capabilities increasingly important for the future workforce in the AI era, including emotional intelligence, communication, creativity, and ethical reasoning.

The work investigates how AI-generated assessment and adaptive feedback can augment rather than replace human learning, professional judgment, and responsibility. Particular attention is given to aligning AI support with educational objectives, disciplinary expectations, and human oversight.

This work addresses future workforce development in the AI era.

4. AI Transformer Models for Bridge Deterioration Prediction and Preventive Maintenance

Three active MarTREC studies form a connected AI infrastructure research sequence. The already published 2026 Temporal Fusion Transformer journal paper remains under Publications only and is not listed here.

Improving Chloride-Induced Bridge Deterioration Forecasting with Physically Associated Supplemental Information

This study investigates whether physically associated supplemental information can improve AI-based forecasting of chloride-induced deterioration in reinforced-concrete bridge infrastructure. Using transformer-based time-series prediction, the research compares prediction performance with and without additional physically meaningful variables to determine when richer information produces measurable gains. Particular attention is given to identifying which deterioration indicators benefit from supplemental information and which do not, providing guidance for efficient and physically interpretable bridge-deterioration modeling.

Physics-Guided Multi-Task Temporal Fusion Transformer for Learning Literature-Derived Bridge Deterioration Trajectories under Sparse and Heterogeneous Observations

This research extends transformer-based bridge-deterioration prediction from controlled simulation toward real experimental observations reported in scientific literature. A literature-data pipeline is being used to retrieve, screen, extract, structure, and temporally align deterioration measurements from heterogeneous studies. The resulting data are used to train and evaluate a physics-guided multi-task Temporal Fusion Transformer for learning deterioration trajectories under sparse, incomplete, and heterogeneous observations. The study establishes a pathway from literature-derived evidence toward real-data predictive modeling.

Integrating Bridge Inspection Data and Scientific Deterioration Knowledge for Real-Bridge Forecasting and Preventive Maintenance

This research is developing a framework for combining longitudinal bridge inspection information with experimental and mechanistic deterioration evidence extracted from scientific literature. The objective is to create temporally aligned bridge-level datasets that support AI-based prediction of real bridge deterioration behavior and ultimately support risk-informed inspection, preventive-maintenance planning, and engineering decision-making under uncertainty.

Research progression: physically enriched prediction → literature-derived real-data learning → real-bridge forecasting → preventive-maintenance decision support

5. Human Evaluation of AI-Generated Assessment and Adaptive Feedback for Open-Ended Student Reasoning

This study evaluates the accuracy, quality, appropriateness, usefulness, and limitations of AI-generated assessment and adaptive feedback for students’ open-ended responses. AI-based assessments and individualized feedback have already been generated from authentic student data using instructor-defined criteria and disciplinary context.

Faculty and domain experts are being engaged to independently evaluate the AI outputs and compare human judgment with AI-generated results. The research examines where AI assessment aligns with expert judgment, where disagreement occurs, and what forms of human oversight are needed for responsible AI-supported learning.

6. AI Agent-Augmented Systematic Review for Synthesizing Evidence to Guide Pedagogical Innovation

Submitted for peer review — AERA 2027.

This study investigates the use of AI agents to augment systematic literature review and evidence synthesis for educational research. The research examines how agent-based workflows can assist with literature identification, evidence extraction, organization, comparison, and synthesis while retaining human oversight of interpretation and research judgment.

The broader objective is to develop a scalable AI-supported research workflow for translating rapidly expanding research evidence into pedagogical innovation.

Emerging Research Development

7. Knowledge-Grounded AI for Human-Centered Disciplinary and Ethical Decision-Making

This emerging research develops a knowledge-grounded AI framework for helping human decision-makers consider multiple stakeholder perspectives, professional principles, cultural context, risks, and competing values.

The proposed architecture combines structured knowledge graphs with retrieval-augmented generation and large language models to surface relevant knowledge and potentially overlooked perspectives while preserving human responsibility for final judgment.

Current development focuses on complementary knowledge structures representing:

  • disciplinary and ethical knowledge;
  • professional principles and cases;
  • stakeholders and risks;
  • human and community perspectives;
  • cultural and contextual differences;
  • competing values and judgments.

Intended workflow: human/community perspectives + disciplinary and ethical knowledge + knowledge graphs + RAG → LLM-supported multi-perspective reasoning → human final judgment

This emerging direction is especially suitable for interdisciplinary and international collaboration.
